Week 1 · with your case manager

Readiness Review

Starting a business can mean clearing a few steps first: ID, debts to DC, a record, credit, supervision rules, and a world that went digital. None of these has to stop you. In week one, you and your case manager go through each one together.

Four things to check in week one

  • ID

    A government photo ID is needed to register a business and open a bank account.

  • Clean Hands

    DC will not license a business if you owe the District more than $100. Check your status on MyTax.DC.gov.

  • Supervision

    If you are on supervision, tell your officer about self-employment and check any limits on social media.

  • Credit and banking

    Pull your free credit report. No history or old debts? Your case manager helps you build a plan.

Nothing you share in your Readiness Review is used to judge you, and you decide what to share.

Built into the lessons

Day 1Day one is about safety and belonging. Nobody is asked to share their story. The Case Management Associate meets each participant this week for a short readiness review (ID, phone, housing, supervision schedule).
Day 2Many participants come home to a different DC. The opportunity scan doubles as a tour of what changed.
Day 3Clean Hands: DC will not issue a business license to anyone who owes the District more than $100 in taxes, fines, or fees. Participants check their status on MyTax.DC.gov early, and case management helps set up payment plans. Child support arrears are handled through the Child Support Services Division.
Day 4Bring a photo ID for building security. Anyone without one tells their case manager by Day 2 so staff can arrange entry. Questions about how a record affects licensing can be asked privately, and the class covers where to get free legal help with record sealing (your instructor confirms current rules with you).
Day 5Staff walk anyone without a working email or phone number through getting one before the lab.
Day 6Approaching strangers can be hard after years away. Scripts and pair practice come first, and participants may start with people they already know.
Day 9Social media has changed a lot. Participants under supervision check whether their conditions limit social media use before opening business accounts. The class covers scams, privacy settings, and keeping a business page separate from a personal one.
Day 10Your story, your terms: participants practice a short, forward-looking way to explain a gap if they choose, and learn they never have to disclose more than the law requires.
Day 11People returning after years away may have no credit history, old debts, or identity theft. Pulling a credit report is private and optional; case management helps dispute errors and find second-chance banking.
Day 12Spreadsheets may be new. Staff pair participants and use a fill-in template so nobody starts from a blank sheet.
Day 14Case management connects participants to opening a business bank account with Truist or another partner.
Day 16AI tools did not exist in this form a few years ago. The lab starts from zero and covers privacy and scams.
Day 17DC's Fair Criminal Record Screening law limits when most employers may ask about records (your instructor confirms current rules with you). Participants practice a short, honest, forward-looking answer about gaps.
Day 19Dress for Success package (attire, headshots, business cards) is delivered before the capstone.
Day 20Graduates keep Canvas access, their reference guides, the GWBCC membership, and follow-up from case management after the program ends.

In crisis or need to talk? Call or text 988, or call the DC Access Helpline at 1-888-793-4357, any time.
