Getting Started

On the first day, we set up your accounts together, go over how the class works, and you take a short pre-assessment. Bring your phone and a photo ID if you have one. Laptops are provided.

Pre-assessment · about 25 minutes

Day One Pre-Assessment

A short check of what you already know about starting and running a business. It shows where you're starting, so the course can help you grow from there. It isn't graded, and it's fine not to know the answers yet. You take the same check on Day 20 to see how far you've come.

  1. Log in to Canvas with the account your instructor sets up with you.
  2. Answer on your own. It measures you, not a search engine or an AI tool.
  3. Click Submit at the end.

Set up your accounts

  • A professional email

    One you're comfortable giving to customers and lenders. If yours is a nickname, we will help you make one with your name.

  • Canvas

    Where you find materials, answer the daily exit ticket (that is how attendance is counted), and submit your projects. You keep access after you graduate.

  • A Google account

    Google Drive keeps your business plan, survey, and spreadsheets in one place. Google Forms runs your market research survey.

  • Your phone

    Charged, with a working number. You'll use it for customer interviews, banking apps, and your business social media.

Missing an ID, a phone, or an email? Come anyway. Your case manager will help you sort it out in your Readiness Review this week.

How each class works

Every class is 3 hours and follows the same rhythm: Learn → Do → Build. Most days start with a 5-minute Tech Catch-Up on one digital skill.

  1. 1. Learn

    Expert Content from your instructor and your IRSJA guide: Boss Up, Money Moves, or Flipping the Game.

  2. 2. Do

    Field Experience in the real market (interviews, surveys, networking) or a Master Class with a guest expert or Truist Bank.

  3. 3. Build

    Add today's piece to your own business portfolio, then answer the exit ticket and click Submit in Canvas.

Your words, your work

Every module project ends with an In your own words section. Your instructor, AI tools, and classmates can help you build your plan, but this part you write yourself. Lenders, partners, and the pitch panel want to know what you understand about your business.

  • “I chose this business structure because …”
  • “I set my price at … because …”

Short and honest beats long and polished.

Ground rules

  • Respect: one voice at a time. Disagree with ideas, not people.
  • Your story, your terms: you never have to share anything about your past.
  • What people share in this room stays in this room.
  • Attendance: miss two days in a row and your case manager reaches out to help, not to judge.
  • Check facts that matter, especially licensing rules and numbers, before you rely on them.
  • Write the “In your own words” part of every project yourself.

Tech Catch-Up

One quick digital skill most days. If you have been away for a while, this is where you catch up. If you already know it, you help someone who doesn't.

  1. Logging in to Canvas and email; strong passwords and two-step login
  2. Saving, finding, and sharing files in Google Drive
  3. Government websites: making an account safely, reading PDFs, and filling in forms
  4. Recording notes and voice memos on your phone
  5. Google Forms for surveys; sharing a link by text
  6. Canva for logos and flyers
  7. Business social media accounts and privacy settings
  8. Banking apps, Zelle and Cash App, and spotting payment scams
  9. Spreadsheet basics: cells, sums, and formulas
  10. AI assistants: what they are good at, and what to check yourself

New to business or to computers, or coming back after time away? That's who this course was built for. Ask questions early and often.
